# Your First Airship

Every captain starts with something wobbly. This guide takes you from a pile of planks to a ship that takes off, flies a lap and lands again. It follows the **Skyward** chapter of the quest book, so you can tick off quests as you go.

> **Golden rule:** hold **W** over any new block to open its Ponder scene. It shows you how the block works, in motion. Seriously, do it.

## Before you start

- Work through **Clockwork** until rotation, stress and the Brass Age feel familiar. Skyward builds on it.
- Craft a pair of **Aviator's Goggles**. They add information about placed flight parts to your HUD.
- Pick a spot with room around it and no base beneath it. First flights end in the trees more often than you think.

## 1. Make it a vessel

The **Physics Assembler** turns a group of connected blocks into a vessel with real physics. Build something, place the assembler on it, power it, and it comes alive. Connected parts merge into one vessel, disconnected parts split into separate ones.

Start with a small raft of planks and give it a push. Use **Honey Glue** (cheap and friendly) or Super Glue to attach blocks that should move together.

## 2. Lift

Your first lift comes from hot air:

- **Envelope blocks** enclose a volume of air. The bigger the enclosed space, the more it lifts. They come in every color.
- A **Hot Air Burner** sits under the opening of the envelope and fills it when powered by redstone. The input panel sets the maximum output, the redstone strength scales it. That is your altitude control. Several burners can feed one balloon.
- If your ship already carries a steam engine, a **Steam Vent** lifts with steam from the boiler instead.
- **Envelope Encased Shafts** and pipes carry rotation and fluids through the balloon without leaking lift.

Later, **Levitite** makes a vessel float without fuel. It needs end stone, so it is a late-game luxury, not a starter part.

## 3. Thrust

- A **Propeller Bearing** turns attached sails or blades into a propeller. Wooden and Andesite Propellers are ready-made heads. Bigger props push more, faster spin pushes harder.
- The **Portable Engine** burns fuel and makes rotation. It is small and light, and the most common way to drive a propeller.

## 4. Steering

- The **Steering Wheel** gives precise rotational output. Combine it with a **Swivel Bearing** to turn a rudder or a whole propeller mount.
- The **Throttle Lever** is a compact, precise redstone source. Hook it to burners and engines and you have a real cockpit.
- **Sails** work as rudders and stabilizers on a moving ship.

## 5. Check before you fly

Open the **Contraption Diagram**. It shows mass, center of mass, lift and thrust. The preflight checklist warns you about missing essentials, bad balance, blocked parts and unstable cargo.

Keep the weight centered under the lift. Most first ships that roll over are simply lopsided.

## 6. Maiden voyage

Take off, fly a lap and land again without falling apart. That is the **Wright Brothers** quest. It does not have to be pretty. The first one never is.

Air gets thinner the higher you climb. Burners, vents and propellers all lose power with altitude, so every ship has a ceiling. Find yours.

## 7. Mooring and landing

- **Rope Connectors** and a **Rope Winch** tie your ship to the ground or to another ship. Reel the rope in to pull the ship down.
- **Docking Connectors** lock two vessels together in pairs. A mothership with shuttles is closer than you think.
- **Handles** give you something to hold on to when you climb over the side.
- Give your ship a **Nameplate**. What is she called?

## Where to go next

- **Easier flying:** Smart Propellers that keep themselves level, gyroscopic bearings that stabilize the whole vessel.
- **Redstone autopilot:** altitude, velocity and gimbal sensors, a Navigation Table that points you home.
- **Ground Control:** control desks, thrusters, flight computers that hold altitude and attitude, recorded routes and airship stations. See [[Vehicles]].

## Common problems

| Problem | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| The ship does not lift off | Not enough envelope volume, or the burner is not powered. Check the diagram. |
| It tips over or spins | Weight is off-center, or the propellers push unevenly. |
| It stops climbing | You reached the ceiling for your burners and props. |
| It looks broken in flight | Shaders. See [[Troubleshooting]]. |
| The game slows down near it | The ship is too big or there are many vessels nearby. See [[Performance and Settings]]. |
